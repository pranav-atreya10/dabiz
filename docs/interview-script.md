# Customer interview script

Use this for 20–30 minute calls with people who lead or work on software teams. The goal is to
learn about their real problems, not to sell the app. Talk less, listen more.

## Before the call
- Know who they are: their job title and team.
- Have one person ask questions and one take notes, or record the call (ask first).
- Have the app open, in case you get to show it at the end.

## 1. Opening (say this first)
> "Thanks for making time. We're two students building a startup. We're looking for problems that
> software teams have, especially turning ideas into something engineers can build. There are no
> right answers, and nothing to buy. We just want to hear how things work for you."

Saying what the call is about first matters. Without it, people don't know what you're asking.

## 2. About them (2 min)
1. What's your job title, and what does your team work on?
2. How many people are on your team? Who do you work with most outside it?

## 3. How work happens today (10 min)
3. When someone has a new idea for a product or feature, what happens next, step by step?
4. Who writes down the requirements? What do they look like (a doc, tickets, slides)?
5. **Tell me about the last time that went badly.** What happened? What did it cost (time, rework)?
6. What tools do you use for this? What do you like and dislike about each?
7. How long does it usually take to go from an idea to work engineers can start on?
8. Has your team built its own tool or process to fix this? What does it do, and what's still missing?

Keep asking "Why?" and "Can you give me an example?" Real stories beat opinions.

## 4. The problem, in their words (5 min)
9. If you could wave a magic wand and fix one part of this, what would it be?
10. Who on your team feels this pain the most?
11. Is this a top-3 problem for your team, or more of an annoyance?

## 5. Show the idea (5 min, only if there's time)
Share your screen and run one idea through the app. Then ask:
12. What's useful here? What's missing or wrong?
13. What would it have to connect to for your team to actually use it?
    *(Let them name tools. Don't suggest any, like "DevOps?", first.)*

## 6. Would they pay? (3 min)
14. If this saved your team that time, would it be worth paying for?
15. Roughly how much would make sense, per person or per team, per month?
16. Who would have to approve that? What would it replace?

A clear yes with a price and a person who'd approve it is a strong sign. "Maybe" or "it could be"
means no for now.

## 7. Wrap up (2 min)
17. Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?
18. Who else should I talk to about this? Could you introduce me?
19. Can we follow up when we have a new version to try?

## After the call (same day)
Write down:
- The most important quote, word for word
- Their biggest pain, in one sentence
- What they use today, and what they've tried
- Pay signal: none / maybe / clear yes (with price)
- Follow-ups you promised, and introductions they offered

## Avoid
- **Leading questions** ("Wouldn't it be great if…?", "DevOps?"). Ask open questions and wait.
- **Talking about your idea too early.** Learn about their world first.
- **Taking polite answers as a yes.** Ask for specifics: numbers, examples, names.
- **Asking two questions at once.** Ask one, then wait for the full answer.

## Follow-ups for the first interview (Pranesh, partner at Microsoft)
- He'll get back to you on what non-technical colleagues struggle with.
- He said "yes and no" about being satisfied with his tools. Which parts?
- His team is building its own tool. What does it do, and what's missing?
- Ask again about paying: how much, and who would approve it?
- He works on hardware. Could he introduce you to software team leads?
